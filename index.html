02The basics

Three terms
you need to know.

College sports money changed fast. Here's what these words mean, in plain English.

NIL
Name, Image & Likeness

Athletes getting paid by brands to use their name or face — sponsorships, ads, social posts, autographs.Example: a local car dealership pays a quarterback for an Instagram post.

Revenue sharing
Schools paying athletes

Colleges paying athletes directly, using money the school earns from sports like TV deals and tickets.New as of the 2025 House settlement.

House settlement
The 2025 court deal

The 2025 legal settlement that ended the old “amateur” rules and allowed schools to pay their athletes.Named after the lawsuit House v. NCAA.

03Research · What changed

Amateurs
to earners.

In just a few years, athletes went from earning nothing to running what is basically a small business — with no training.

Before

No pay. No safety net.

Athletes were labeled “amateurs.” They couldn't be paid, and they couldn't get workers' comp if they got hurt.

Gale
2025

Schools can pay players.

The House settlement ended “the tradition of amateurism in US college sports.” Schools can now pay athletes directly.

Today

Taxes are the athlete's job.

NIL money is taxable income. Nobody takes taxes out automatically — athletes have to report it themselves.

06Answering our question

What we
found.

Putting the research together, here's our answer to the essential question.

How · Decisions

Athletes make big money choices alone.

They sign deals and handle taxes themselves, with almost no one guiding them — and they're overconfident about it.

What · Money

The money is real, the training isn't.

Schools paid $1.77B in year one, but only 9% of athletes in one study had ever seen a financial counselor.

Why · Health & future

That pressure turns into stress.

More NIL means more stress, and bad money habits can follow athletes long after their playing days end.

07Mission statement

Beyond the Game gives college athletes free financial coaching so NIL money becomes security, not stress.

Our vision

Every college athlete leaves school with their health, their money, and a plan for life after sports.

Who we help

College athletes earning NIL or revenue-sharing money — starting the day they sign their first deal.

08Plan for change

Our plan
in three steps.

Each step fixes a problem our research found.

Step 01

Teach the basics

Short workshops that fit team schedules. Topics: budgeting, paying taxes on NIL income, and reading a contract before signing it.

Fixes: athletes have to report NIL taxes themselves, and know less about money than they think.
Step 02

Give every athlete a money coach

Each athlete is matched with a free, one-on-one financial coach they can text before signing any deal.

Fixes: only 9% of athletes in one study had ever met with a financial counselor.
Step 03

Check in on stress

Money coaching is paired with regular mental health check-ins, so stress from deals gets caught early.

Fixes: more NIL commitment is linked to more stress.
